# DRON — Voice

How the product speaks. One page. Every product string is checked against it (with `microcopy.md`).

**Stance.** DRON moves parcels, documents structures, and sends a stranger with a flying machine to your home — and takes your money online to do it. In that context **trust beats wit, every line.** The voice is calm, specific, and accountable. It states facts, not adjectives; outcomes, not equipment; and in trouble it tells you what happened and what it costs you — it never performs cheerfulness. Not advertising. Concrete.

**Derived from:** `CLAUDE.md` · `research/research.md` (incl. *Competitor Language*) · `research/target-audience.md` (personas) · `research/jtbd.md` · supporting: `ux-research/benchmarks.md`. Naming/tone cross-references point at `microcopy.md`.
Rule: **no principle without a line of data behind it.** Better three honest than five pretty — these five each carry their source.

---

## Principles

### P1 — Name the outcome, not the drone
**Rule.** The product speaks in the result the client gets — a parcel delivered, a roof documented, footage ready — and keeps the drone in the background; it never sells the flight as a spectacle.

**Example (per the rule)**
- `Package delivery — parcels, documents, small goods, across the city.`
- `Delivered. Your parcel arrived at 10:07.`

**Anti-example (breaks it)**
- `Look up — your delivery is inbound from the sky! 🚁`
- `Experience the future of delivery. Watch it fly.`

**Why (from data).** MJ-1 (`jtbd.md`): the client wants to *"get the outcome without ever thinking about the drone or the operator."* Persona C-1's own title (`target-audience.md`): *"I want it done. I don't want to think about it."* And the drone is the **anxiety source, not the hook** — `research.md` Finding 3: the top fears are the drone itself (privacy, safety, noise); *"more than half of US respondents reported little or no trust in drones."* Every competitor does the opposite (Competitor Language **L-1**: Wing *"Delivery, elevated"*, Zipline *"Look up"*, Manna *"From the sky. Seriously."*) — spotlighting the drone amplifies the exact fear that makes C-1 leave.

---

### P2 — Show the proof, not the adjective
**Rule.** DRON never calls itself or its operators *professional / trusted / expert / vetted* — it shows the specific, checkable credential (name, licence, insurance, job count) and lets the user draw the conclusion.

**Example (per the rule)**
- `Verified by DRON · CAA licence · Insured · 214 jobs`
- New operator, honestly: `Kateryna O. — licence-checked and insured by DRON. Reviews build from here.`

**Anti-example (breaks it)**
- `Our trusted, professional, hand-vetted expert pilots.`
- `The best drone team in Kyiv!`

**Why (from data).** RJ-C1 (`jtbd.md`) — verified badge + insurance on the list card *without a tap* — is *"the highest single drop-off in the CJM."* C-1 trust triggers (`target-audience.md`): *"'Verified by DRON' + insurance badge visible before payment"* builds trust; *"a card with no photo or '0 reviews'"* triggers exit. Benchmark M-3 (`benchmarks.md`, Zocdoc): credentials precede the bio. And `research.md`'s common pattern: *"Quantified credibility over claims… Numbers do what 'trusted' and 'reliable' cannot."* The whole field asserts the adjective and hides the proof (Competitor Language **L-2**: *"vetted and proven"*, *"certified and experienced pilots"* — with, per Finding 1, no licence or insurance actually shown).
**Vocabulary:** one name for this person — **operator** (the term `jtbd.md`/`benchmarks.md`/product docs use), never *pilot* (the competitor-generic word). See `microcopy.md` **O1**.

---

### P3 — State the price as a fact, before the tap
**Rule.** The full price appears before any commitment, locked, with no *from*, *quote*, *negotiable*, or *plus fees*.

**Example (per the rule)**
- `Total · locked, no extra fees — ₴180`
- `This is the final price. Nothing is added at checkout.`

**Anti-example (breaks it)**
- `From ₴180*` · `Get a quote`
- `Final price depends on the job — contact us.`

**Why (from data).** RJ-C3 (`jtbd.md`): *"the full cost upfront — no estimates, no 'plus fees'."* CJM Drop-off #2: *"any 'plus fees' or surprise on the payment screen = abandonment"* (Whalen: hidden cost is a *rage-quit* trigger). C-1 exit trigger (`target-audience.md`): *"any 'plus fees' or line-item surprise."* The whole market gates price (Competitor Language **L-3**: *"Get a Quote"*, *"Custom"*, *"Договірна"* [negotiable], ABD Video *"Вартість роботи залежить від…"* [cost depends on…] + *"call this number"*; Findings 1 & 5). Stating the number *is* the differentiator.

---

### P4 — Speak the user's words, not the equipment
**Rule.** Describe the job in plain outcomes the client already uses ("roof inspection", "aerial photo"), never in drone hardware or industry jargon (UAV, orthomosaic, VTOL, Mavic 3T, payload).

**Example (per the rule)**
- `Roofs, facades, hard-to-reach structures.`
- `We'll send you a photo the moment it's done.`

**Anti-example (breaks it)**
- `6K UAV orthomosaic capture · DJI Mavic 3T thermal payload.`
- `Schedule your autonomous BVLOS sortie.`

**Why (from data).** Persona C-1 (`target-audience.md`): *"They don't understand how drones work and don't intend to learn"* — with a Bolt/Glovo mental model; *"if the experience diverges from that model in the first 60 seconds, they leave."* Explicit C-1 exit trigger: *"Drone jargon (UAV, payload, LiPo) — creates distance."* `CLAUDE.md` sets the bar: Apple HIG (mobile) and WCAG AA (desktop) — plain, human language. The field talks hardware to non-technical buyers (Competitor Language **L-4**: *"unmanned platforms with 4K/6K cameras"*, *"orthomosaics"*, *"3D twins"*, *"тепловізійне обстеження DJI Mavic 3T"*). *(This also rules out keyword-stuffing — the OLX synonym pile — which P4 and the GEO section below both reject.)*

---

### P5 — In trouble, be plain and accountable — not cheery
**Rule.** When something fails — money, delay, missing proof — the product states what happened, what it means for the user's money, and the next step; no "Oops", no exclamation marks, no forced optimism.

**Example (per the rule)**
- `Payment declined. Nothing was taken from your account. Try again or use another method.`
- `Running a few minutes late — traffic near the drop-off. Your operator is still on the way.`

**Anti-example (breaks it)**
- `Oops! Something went wrong 😅 Don't worry, we're on it!`
- `You're all set! 🎉 Almost there — keep going!`

**Why (from data).** EJ-2 (`jtbd.md`): the client needs *"a clear path to resolution — not just a rating I can leave"*; CJM Stage 3 flags *"one bad experience = permanent churn"* 🔴. Benchmarks (`benchmarks.md`): Airbnb AirCover scores first-use anxiety ★★★★★ precisely by being a calm, concrete guarantee; Glovo's *"no-questions refund"* is stated flatly, never celebrated. This is the *trust-over-wit* context in its sharpest form: at a money-or-safety moment, cheer reads as evasion. Our own copy already slips here — `microcopy.md` **TONE** flags *"You're all set"* and *"Almost there / KEEP GOING"* — this principle is the fix.

## Vocabulary — one concept, one word

Every divergence flagged in `microcopy.md` (Step 01), resolved. **One word per concept, the same on every screen.** Terms are taken from the language of the personas and research — none invented. "Why" is written in the persona's own words, not office-speak.

One name; see `P2`. |

| A10 | go back to a section | **Back to {Section}** using its nav label (**Back to Activity / Back to Jobs**) | ~~Back to orders~~, mixed nouns | The button names the place by the same word that place is called in the nav (sitemap §7.1) — see O3. |

### Form of address — fixed, every screen

- **English (primary):** direct second person — **"you"**, always. Never third person ("the user"), never corporate ("customers should…"). *Why:* C-1 arrives from Bolt/Glovo, apps that talk *to you*, plainly — trust comes from a direct human register, not distance.
- **Ukrainian (secondary):** informal singular — **«ти»**, the same on every screen; never «ви», never mixed. *Why:* it matches the Bolt/Glovo consumer register C-1 already lives in. *(Deliberate call: Diia itself uses formal «ви» — DRON does not follow it; we pick one register, «ти», and hold it everywhere.)*

### Anglicisms — what's allowed in the Ukrainian copy

- **Allowed** (users search and say these; no better native word): **дрон** (the actual search term — `research.md`: *"замовити дрон"*, OLX/Kabanchik *"дрон / квадрокоптер"*); brand & ID names **Diia, BankID, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Visa/Mastercard**; **онлайн**.
- **Not allowed** (say it in Ukrainian): ~~кешаут / пейаут~~ → **вивести кошти / платіж**; ~~сабмітити~~ → **надіслати**; ~~трекати~~ → **відстежувати**; and never the hardware jargon of `P4` (~~UAV, БПЛА, payload, orthomosaic~~) in consumer copy — say **дрон**, **фото**, **звіт**.

## Don't use — never write this

Each row: the pattern we ban, and a real fix. "Was" quotes come from our own `microcopy.md` **TONE** flags where we already slipped — this is the fix list, not a hypothetical one.

### AI clichés (the reflex phrases)

| Never write | Was → Should be |
|---|---|
| "Oops, something went wrong" / «Ой, щось пішло не так» | ~~"Oops! Something went wrong."~~ → **"Payment declined. Nothing was taken from your account. Try again or use another method."** (`P5`: name what happened + the money + the next step) |
| "You're all set" (empty filler before the fact) | ~~"You're all set"~~ *(order-confirmed)* → **"Payment received. Your operator is on the way."** (the fact was already the next line — lead with it) |
| "Welcome!" / "Congratulations!" / «Ласкаво просимо» / «Вітаємо!» | ~~"Welcome to DRON!"~~ → **"You order, a pro handles it."** (say what DRON does, not hello) |
| chatty hedges — "let's sort it out", "no questions asked", "Hold on a moment" | ~~"…the job can't be paid — let's sort it out."~~ *(inspection-report-error)* → **"…the job can't be completed or paid. Contact support to resolve it."** |

### Motivational / journey tone

| Never write | Was → Should be |
|---|---|
| aspirational journey copy — "begin your journey to new possibilities" / «розпочни свій шлях до нової можливості» | ~~"Start your journey with DRON"~~ → **"Browse services"** / **"Order your first drone service."** (a plain CTA, not a mood) |
| cheerleading — "Almost there!", "Keep going!" | ~~"Almost there / KEEP GOING"~~ *(wallet-empty)* → **"₴500 needed to withdraw. One more job reaches it."** (`P5`: the number, not a pep talk) |
| filler headings — "The short version" | ~~"The short version"~~ *(onboarding-client-empty)* → **"How DRON works"** (or just show the three facts) |

### Exclamations, emoji, and "successfully"

| Never write | Was → Should be |
|---|---|
| exclamation marks in system / status / error copy | ~~"Payment failed!"~~ → **"Payment declined. Nothing was taken from your account."** |
| emoji in system messages (a rating ★ is data, not emoji) | ~~"Order confirmed 🎉"~~ → **"Order confirmed. Your operator is on the way."** |
| the word **"successfully"** / «успішно» | ~~"Payment completed successfully!"~~ → **"Payment received. ₴180 charged to Visa ···· 4921."** (a fact is proof; "successfully" adds nothing) |
| "How was it?" and other cute prompts | ~~"How was it?"~~ *(rate)* → **"Rate this order."** |

**One line to remember:** if a string cheers, hedges, or decorates, it is hiding the absence of a fact. Delete it and state the fact.

## GEO & SEO — how this voice gets found

The discipline that builds trust here — **specific facts, plain outcome words, one name per thing** — is the same discipline that makes DRON the result a search engine ranks and the answer an LLM quotes. Voice and findability are one problem, not two.

### How the user actually searches their pain
People do not search *"drone-as-a-service platform."* They search the **outcome + place + worry**, and increasingly they *ask an assistant* in a full sentence:
- **C-1 (urgent client):** *"замовити дрон Київ"* [order a drone, Kyiv] (`target-audience.md`), *"drone delivery Kyiv"*, *"send documents across the city fast"* → and to an LLM: *"Can I get a parcel delivered by drone in Kyiv?"*
- **C-2 (planning client):** the mood-quote *is* the query — *"Is this actually available where I live?"* (`target-audience.md`); plus *"aerial photography Kyiv price"*, *"how much does a drone roof inspection cost"*.
- **The anxiety queries (`research.md` Finding 3):** *"is drone delivery safe"*, *"are delivery drones noisy"*, *"drones and privacy"*. Whoever answers these **calmly and specifically becomes the cited source.**
- **UA real search terms (Competitor Language):** operators and buyers use *"аерозйомка"*, *"зйомка з дрона"*, *"квадрокоптер"* — DRON's plain service names must map to these intents in **both** EN and UK.
- **Operators (O-1):** *"another platform that takes 30%?"* (`target-audience.md`) → *"drone operator jobs Ukraine"*, *"what commission does [platform] take"*.

### What to do about it (GEO = being the answer, not just a blue link)
1. **Put the real question on the page, answered plainly.** Build FAQ blocks that mirror the pain-queries verbatim — *"Is drone delivery safe?" · "Do I need to know anything about drones?" · "How much does it cost?" · "Is DRON available in my city?"* LLMs lift question→answer pairs; if the question is not on a page, DRON cannot be the answer.
2. **Lead with quotable facts, not adjectives** (this is P2/P3 and Competitor **L-5**, the field's one good habit). *"₴180, final price" · "covers Kyiv city" · "CAA-licensed, insured operators" · "photo proof within 15 min" · "auto-refund if no operator is found."* AI Overviews and chat assistants quote numbers and skip *"trusted, professional."* The trust sentence and the GEO payload are the same sentence.
3. **Make the facts machine-readable** — schema.org JSON-LD: `Service`, `Offer` (price + currency), `LocalBusiness`/`areaServed` (coverage cities), `FAQPage`, and **real** `AggregateRating`/`Review`. This is what Google AI Overviews and Gemini extract; keep every number honest (`research.md` already caught a *fabricated* insurance figure — same rule).
4. **One name per entity** (P2's vocabulary rule, `microcopy.md` O1/O3). Calling it *order* here and *job* there, *operator* here and *pilot* there, degrades retrieval and entity resolution — an assistant can't confidently attach the facts to one thing. Consistency is a voice rule *and* a ranking rule.
5. **Mirror everything bilingually** (EN primary, UK secondary — `CLAUDE.md`). The pain is searched in both languages; the Ukrainian pages must carry the real terms (*"аерозйомка"*, *"доставка дроном Київ"*) mapped to DRON's plain service names.
6. **Headings = the intent the user typed.** `H1: Order a drone delivery in Kyiv`, `H2: Roof inspection by a certified drone operator — from ₴X, photo report included` — not *"DRON: the future of autonomous aerial logistics."*

### Per-engine notes (different LLMs surface differently)
- **Google AI Overviews / Gemini** — reward schema + genuine E-E-A-T + real reviews. Never fabricate a rating or a coverage claim.
- **ChatGPT / Perplexity** — crawl clean, self-contained text; each **service page and each coverage-city page must answer fully on its own** (several competitor sites were JS-gated and un-fetchable — do not be that page).
- **Claude / task assistants** — answer the *"how do I / is it safe / how much"* questions in plain prose; that phrasing is what gets surfaced when someone asks the assistant to help.
- **Anti-pattern (all engines):** the OLX keyword pile — *"зйомка з дрона, аерозйомка, квадрокоптер, відеозйомка, відео 4K"* — is penalized *and* breaks P4. Plain outcome phrasing wins on both trust and rank.

**The convergence:** say the specific true thing, in plain words, once, and name it the same way everywhere. That single sentence is simultaneously the most trustworthy form and the most quotable one — which is the whole reason voice and GEO are the same job for DRON.

## Microcopy — rules by element type

The last section. Rules for each kind of UI string, one real DRON example each. Every rule is checked against the **Principles** and the **Vocabulary** above — the "Checks" column names which. Screens and states referenced are the real ones in `wireframes/_screens.md`.

| Element | Rule | DRON example | Checks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Button** | An action verb whose result is visible in the label — never a bare noun; use the canonical verb from the Vocabulary. | `View details` — not `Details`. And `Pay ₴180` / `Withdraw ₴3,240` — the verb carries the exact outcome. | A1–A10 · P3 · P4 |
| **Nav-bar back button** | Apple HIG: the chevron + the **title of the screen you return to** — `‹ {previous screen}`; never `Back to …` (the chevron already means "back"). **One screen, one name** — the string in the back button is the destination's name *character for character*, and a screen has exactly one name everywhere it is referred to. That name is resolved in this order: **(1)** the destination's nav-bar title, if it has one; **(2)** its **tab label**, when the destination is a tab root (a tab root shows a brand mark, not a title, so the tab label *is* its name — and the tab label and any nav-bar title on that screen must therefore be the same word); **(3)** bare `‹ Back`, only when the destination has neither — a title-less onboarding slider. A **tab-root** screen itself has no back button (you reach it by switching tabs). *(In-content recovery buttons in empty/error blocks are a different element — content CTAs like `Back to Jobs` — and stay descriptive.)* | `‹ Order review` (on Payment) · `‹ Sign in` (on Verification) · `‹ Order` (on Package delivery — tab label, `listings` has no title) · `‹ Back` (on Operator terms — the onboarding slider has no name) — never `‹ Back to Order review` | HIG (mobile client) · P4 · `microcopy.md` D1 · D8 |
| **Screen heading** | Name the place in the dictionary word for it, bound to the persona — the outcome, not the drone. | `Order confirmed` (client) · `New job` (operator) — not `Success!` or `Your drone is on its way`. | O1–O3 · P1 |
| **Form field — label** | Says exactly *what* to enter, as a noun from the dictionary. | `Drop-off address` — not `Address 2`. | O-vocab · P4 |
| **Form field — hint** | Says *how* — the format, by real example; never just repeats the label. | `e.g. UA-CAA-102938` (under *Licence number*) · `Floor, entrance code, what's inside…` (under *Notes for the operator*). | P4 |
| **Form field — validation error** | Names *exactly what to fix* and how; states the fact, never blames the user. | `Outside the service zone. DRON delivers within Kyiv city — enter an address in the city.` — not `Invalid input`. | P5 · P3 · P4 |
| **Empty state** | Say *why* it's empty (the fact), then the one action out. No pep talk. | `No orders yet. Your orders appear here, ready to re-book in a tap.` + button `Browse services`. | O3 · A4 · P5 · *Don't-use* |
| **Error** | What happened + what it means for the user's money/state + the next action. No apology, no joke, no `!`. | `Payment declined. Nothing was taken from your account. Try again or use another method.` + `Try again`. | P5 · A1 · A2 · *Don't-use* |
| **Loading** | Name the specific thing being fetched — never a bare "Loading…". | `Finding your operator…` — not `Loading…`. (`operator`, never `pilot`.) | O1 · P4 |
| **Success** | Lead with the fact (the number/time), then the next step. No "successfully", no celebration, no emoji. Only the five real success screens earn one. | `Delivered. Your parcel arrived at 10:07.` + `Rate this order`. | P5 · *Don't-use* · `_screens.md` (success is deliberately few) |
| **Dangerous action** | *Before* the tap, name the exact consequence and what cannot be undone, in plain words; the confirm button repeats the verb, never "OK/Yes". | `Decline this job? It goes to another operator and won't come back to you.` + `Decline` / `Keep job`. | O2 · P5 · *Don't-use* |

**How to use the whole file.** Read the string's job → pick the element rule here → write it in the Vocabulary word → check it against the five Principles → make sure it isn't on the Don't-use list. If it cheers, hedges, or decorates, cut it and state the fact.

**voice.md is complete.** From here, every product string — in `microcopy.md` and in the build — is written and checked against this file.
