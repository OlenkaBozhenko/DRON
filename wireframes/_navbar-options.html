The nav bar reads as two names

You are right, and here is why. Measured on ten painted screens: the back label and the title are the same colour (#1A1A1A) at the same weight (600), 2px apart in size. Of the four things that can rank two words — size, weight, colour, position — only one is in use. So they read as two names of equal standing rather than a control and a screen name.

This is not the "one screen, one name" rule failing. That rule is about a screen being called the same thing everywhere, and it holds: 43 of 44 controls pass. A nav bar legitimately carries two different screens — the one you came from and the one you are on. The defect is that nothing tells you which is which. Every bar below is the real ui/kit.css tokens at production size, on the screen you flagged and on the worst pair in the set.

Question 2 — do we also centre the title? This one has a price.

HIG · Navigation bars centres the title. Ours never is: it flows after the back label, so it drifts from −82.7px to +30.4px off centre depending on how long that label happens to be. Centring it is correct — but at 375px the two do not both fit, and iOS's own answer is to truncate the back label. That cuts a screen's name, which is the rule you just set.
